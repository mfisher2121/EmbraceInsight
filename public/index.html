02 / CONTRACTOR PLAYBOOK

The AC failed.
The furnace still works.

Name all three options, in the same order, every time. Say what each one replaces, what it keeps, and what you have to check before you can quote it. Then let the homeowner choose.

Read the playbook guide ↗
OPTION 1

New AC

Replace the outdoor AC and usually the coil. The furnace, ducts and gas service stay. Check the coil matches the furnace.

OPTION 2

Heat pump with furnace backup

The AC becomes a heat pump that heats most of the winter. The furnace takes over when it's coldest. Check the controls.

OPTION 3

Full switch

A heat pump and an air handler with electric backup replace both. Check the load calculation, the panel and the ducts.

No savings promises. Operating cost depends on rates, equipment, the house and how it's run.

A FEW GOOD QUESTIONS

Clear context.
Clear expectations.

Is this a lead list?+

No. The reports publish counts and patterns from public records. They don't name addresses, homeowners or contractors, and a permit count is not a reason to contact anyone.

Where does the data come from?+

The first briefing uses Montgomery County's Mechanical Permits dataset and the Census Bureau's American Community Survey. The briefing's source list gives each retrieval date and checksum.

Why no lead costs or close rates?+

Because we don't have data we can cite for them yet. We'll publish those, and any comparison between named companies, only when we do.

Is this a government website?+

No. Embrace Insight is an independent publication. We link to the public sources so you can check every figure. We don't claim government endorsement or privileged access.

Is there an email list?+

Not yet. When there is, signing up will be a separate, clear choice. For now, write to max@maxfisher.net.
