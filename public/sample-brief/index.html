FOUR-ZIP MARKET BRIEF / SAMPLE FORMAT

Four ZIP codes. One question. A useful brief.

By Max Fisher · Draft for review · 2026-10-29

This sample shows the structure of a Four-ZIP Market Brief. It contains no findings about a real business, market, or customer.

The decision

Which part of the discovery-to-inquiry journey deserves attention first for one HVAC service in four chosen ZIP codes?

01 / Define the territory

  • Research areaFour ZIP codes the business wants to investigate. Service coverage, travel time, and capacity need confirmation.
  • Service focusOne service, such as heat pump installation or maintenance, rather than every service at once.
  • Business questionChoose local visibility or the inquiry journey. That question determines what to observe.

02 / Keep an evidence log

For each observation, record the ZIP code, source URL or authorized internal record, capture date, search or device context, what was seen, and what is still unknown. A screenshot or a claim without context is not a completed finding.

Illustrative observation format
Question: Can a visitor find a clear contact route for the chosen service?
Source: [business page URL]
Checked: [date, device, and steps]
Observation: [what was actually visible]
Status: Evidence to collect
Next verification: [what needs to be tested or confirmed]

03 / Name one next action

After reviewing the evidence, state the finding, why it matters to the selected question, who owns the next action, and when to check the result. Keep estimates and proposed changes separate from observed outcomes.

The research references

Google Business Profile guidance provides context for local visibility. The SBA’s market research guidance distinguishes questions about location, demand, and competition. The four-ZIP boundary is Embrace Insight’s proposed scoping method, not an official ranking rule.
