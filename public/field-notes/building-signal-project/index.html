A building is a signal. A project is a conversation.

By Max Fisher · Draft for review · 2026-10-29

A public building record can help you decide what to investigate. It cannot tell you, by itself, that the owner wants an HVAC project or is ready to buy.

Keep the observation intact.

Start with the exact building identity, source, reporting period, and date checked. Preserve building-ID suffixes and separate multiple buildings on the same parcel. If a number is missing or a target conflicts, make the gap visible.

Ask what is still unknown.

Our research process does not label compliance, replacement need, budget approval, or procurement status from a performance gap alone. Those questions remain unverified until they have separate supporting evidence. Current ownership, the decision route, system condition, and project intent need their own evidence.

Qualify the work and the fit.

Before making an introduction, confirm the need with the owner or an authorized representative. Establish project stage, scope, timing, and permission to share details. Then check comparable contractor experience, technical capability, capacity, and availability.

The next useful action may be a question, a document request, or an engineering assessment. A qualified next step is more useful than a confident guess.

Public research starting point: Montgomery County’s official building benchmarking data ↗. Check the applicable official requirements and building record before drawing conclusions.
